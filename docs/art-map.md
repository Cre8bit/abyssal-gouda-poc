# The Map — visual bible

Companion to `plan-mvp.md` (what gets built) and `cheese-parts.md` (the numbers
that build it). **This document is for the eye.** It describes the run from the
bell to the heart as a sequence of images, so that concept art, materials and
lighting can be produced without re-deriving them from generator parameters.

Everything here is downstream of decisions in `idea-register.md`; the tags in
brackets point back at them.

---

## 0. The rules of the look

**The abyss is made of cheese, and nobody explains why.** [L2] The Golden Gouda
is a duplicator that never stopped running, so the world is a single organism of
cheese with an **age gradient**: the outside is centuries dead — bleached, dry,
crusted, colonised — and the centre is warm, wet, and still being made. Every
material decision should be readable as *how far from the heart is this*.

**Five colours, spent on traits, not on zones.** [U7] Fog eats everything else.
The whole map is built from these and their neutrals:

| Colour | Hex | Means | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| bone / ash | `#CFC8BA` → `#8A8579` | dead cheese, harmless | the drift |
| near-black rind | `#1A1714` → `#3B322A` | the wall you cannot dig | the Great Wheel |
| cold vein blue | `#4FB8C8` → `#1E3A5F` | route, and the only light | the dark veins, both phases |
| molten orange | `#FF8A2B` → `#B04A10` | heat, refuge, and burns | the melt |
| warm tan | `#C69B5E` → `#7A5A32` | the labyrinth, endless and same | the galleries |
| **gold-white** | `#FFE9A8` / `#FFF6DE` | *the prize, and nothing else* | the heart, the Gouda |

Two player-made colours cut across all of it and must never be confused with the
world: **light-stick lime** `#C6FF4A` (chemical, flat, no falloff character) and
**helmet-lamp white** `#FFF4E2` (a hard cone, always the wrong length).

**Scale rhythm.** [W14] `small → colossal → medium → small → colossal →
intimate`. Scale contrast does as much work as material contrast and costs
nothing. Every biome needs one object in frame that establishes it: a rat is
~0.6 u across, a tunnel is ~1 u, a chamber is 8–20 u, the Wheel is **466 u**.

**Light is scarce and always motivated.** Sunlight does not exist. There are
five light sources in the entire game — helmet lamps, light sticks, the veins,
the melt, and the Gouda — and each has a distinct colour, falloff and behaviour.
If something is lit, name what lit it.

**No UI in any concept art.** [D4] No markers, no map, no compass except the
diegetic one. The player's only navigational aids are silhouettes [N9] and the
light sticks they spent.

**Everything is underwater.** The whole map is submerged. There is no walking, no
dry cave, no air-filled space anywhere. Even the heart is an underwater chamber
with an air pocket inside it — a bubble in the water, not a room. Every sound is
muffled by water, every movement is swimming, every light is a cone through
suspended particles.

**Everything is chewed.** Mite bores, gnaw marks, mould bloom, crack webs. This
world was eaten, not built. The one exception is the heart, which is being
made.

## 1. The run, start to centre

Ten beats. Each is a frame worth drawing.

### Beat 1 — Inside the bell
Four rats in brass helmets in a cramped iron bathyscaphe. The only light is a
caged bulb. On the wall you wake up facing: **chalk** [L3] — a crude map, a list
of rules in four different hands, "DON'T EAT IT" scratched out three times,
tally marks for crews that came back. Warm, dry, orange-lit, claustrophobic. It
is the last comfortable image in the run.

### Beat 2 — The berth
Hatch opens. Black water. The bell hangs from nothing, its own lamp making a
small pool of light on the hull, and beyond it: nothing at all, in every
direction. Scale: **intimate**.

### Beat 3 — The drift
Pale chunks of dead cheese tumbling slowly out of the dark, far apart, lit only
by helmet lamps sweeping past them. Somewhere in here, a **wrecked bell** [W10]
with a dead diver still in the harness and a **driller** in his hands. Scale:
**small**, and it teaches you the size of things right before it lies to you.

### Beat 4 — The Wheel appears
The money shot. [D1] The lamps pick up a curve that doesn't end. Pan up, pan
down — it goes past the fog in both directions. It is not a wall; it's an
**object**, a classic wheel of cheese 466 units across, lying tilted in the
abyss, its flat face turned partly toward you so the concentric mould ridges
read as an impossible bullseye. Scale: **colossal**. This is the frame the whole
game is sold on.

### Beat 5 — The soft spots, and the breach
A search along a horizon. Multiple patches where the near-black rind has gone
**wet** — discoloured, swollen, pale paste showing through the crack web, fine
veins converging into them, mite pits scattered like a rash [N2]. The crew
chooses one. Twenty seconds of the loudest noise in the game, chips and dust
boiling off in the lamp beams, and a hole one rat wide [N1] punched through.
Single file, through.

### Beat 6 — The dark void: the lanterns
Inside the husk, and it is **pitch black**. [D2b] No ambient light, no floor, no
horizon, no sense of the space at all — the lamp beam finds nothing to land on
except the water itself. What you see instead: **a scattered constellation of
faint blue lights in the dark**, small, cold, and far apart, arranged in a loose
line that deepens away from you. They are veins glowing inside floating chunks
of roquefort. Behind you, the inner face of the crust arcs away, pale and
**uniformly gnawed** in every direction — visually busy and completely
featureless [W18], which is why your breach hole is gone the moment you turn
around unless somebody spent a light stick on it [D24].

### Beat 7 — Following the lanterns
The trail. You swim to a chunk, and from it you can just make out the next one.
Each is a dark rock lit from within by a blue filigree, hanging in nothing. They
get **bigger and closer together** as you go deeper — first fist-sized glows at
the edge of sight, then boulders, then house-sized masses whose vein networks
light your whole crew as you pass. The chunks slowly **rotate on their own axes**,
so the glowing edges turn away into darkness and come back. Nobody tells you
which way is in; the trail simply tightens and the darkness pulses. Scale:
**small → medium**, deliberately climbing toward something.

### Beat 8 — The shell
The trail ends at something that fills the fog in every direction — a vast
curved wall of dark rind, scorched and vitrified, receding in every direction,
featureless except for deep heat-cracks bleeding orange light from within. It
cannot be drilled anywhere. The crew has found a wall with no soft spots, no way
through except the one the veins lead to: a recessed crevice visible only at
close range, the last vein-glows converging into it. Scale: **colossal**, then
suddenly **intimate** — the only landmarks are the ones the trail itself gives
you.

### Beat 9 — Finding the entrance
The crevice opens. You go through, and from here to the heart you are **within**
the cheese [D2]. The water is thick, hot, and the walls are a different colour:
soft, golden, vent-cooked. Lamps reach further than the veins did but still not
far. Scale: **intimate**, ascending.

### Beat 10 — The melt
A bore opens into a void the water doesn't leave — you're still fully submerged,
but the space explodes: a cavern forty units across, hot, orange, **lit from
below** by vents in the floor, with ropes of molten cheese billowing off the
ceiling and dissolving into the current like ink, pooling in bright roiling
patches. You can see the far wall. You can see every teammate at once. This is
the last generous air-pocket refill [S10] — seeded around the chamber floor,
breathing holes in the limestone-like cooled floes. Scale: **colossal** — and the
only warm, loud place in the whole run.

### Beat 11 — The galleries
Into the tight, tan water again, into a sponge: thousands of bores through
warm cheese, one-rat squeezes opening without warning into rooms where four lamps
point at four different tunnels, every surface identical in every direction. You
navigate by the light sticks you had the sense to drop. You are not lost; the
map you made is the only thing between the crew and becoming the map. Scale:
**small**, unrelenting.

### Beat 12 — The heart
A bore ends and the wall ahead is **white and wet**, and behind it a rounded
chamber still forming, walls of soft curd, whey beading and running, warm light
emanating from the centre. An underwater chamber, not a dry cave — the last air
pocket floats near the ceiling, a bubble in the water — and in the very centre,
on the bottom where it rests, the only pure gold in the world. Four exhausted
rats in the doorway, weightless, exhausted, finally still. Scale: **intimate**.

Then you pick it up, and everything goes wrong [C4].

## 2. The stops, in detail

Each stop lists the frame, the palette, the light, the parts in it, and what
*not* to draw.

---

### ▸ THE DRIFT — *bleached rind*
`r 300 → 240 · water · scatter · part: drift crumb`

**What it is.** The harmless baseline. Nothing here can hurt you and nothing
here is a wall. Its job is to establish scale honestly, so the Wheel can break
it.

**The frame.** Wide, mostly empty. Three or four pale chunks at different
distances, a rat silhouetted against the nearest one, the wreck at the edge of
lamp range with one light still weakly on.

**Palette.** Bone `#CFC8BA`, ash `#8A8579`, against water black `#05070A`.
Helmet-lamp cones are the only warmth.

**Light & fog.** Standard: lamp range nominal, fog thick enough that a chunk is
silhouette-only past ~40 m. Nothing is lit that a rat didn't light.

**The parts.**
- **Drift crumb** — irregular blocks 8–16 u, sea-glass-rounded edges, matte,
  faintly powdery, zero specular. Shallow round eyes and pores, no interior worth
  entering. Each tumbles slowly on its own axis, none in sync. Reference: sun-
  bleached driftwood and chalk cliff fall, not cheese-shop cheese.
- **The wreck** [W10, D23] — an older bell, split, half its hull gone, a diver
  still in the harness with the driller. The set dressing that carries the lore.

**Don't draw.** Anything that reads as architecture, or a chunk big enough to be
a landmark. Nothing in the drift may compete with Beat 4.

### ▸ THE GREAT WHEEL — *dark rind* · **the first gate**
`r 236 → 230 · 6 u thick · hull wheel · part: dark rind`

**What it is.** One classic wheel of cheese, 466 units across, aged in the dark
for centuries and **eaten hollow from within** [D2b] — a husk with six metres
of crust left. The first gate: a horizon that doesn't stop, and the crew must
find one of **several scattered soft spots** to breach it.

**The frame — exterior.** Approach from three-quarters so the flat face and the
rim are both visible. The **concentric ridges of the mould** on the flat face,
turning slowly out of the fog. Rats are dust-motes against it. Lamp beams die
long before they reach the top of the curve.

**Palette.** Near-black `#1A1714` at distance, resolving up close into deep
grey-brown mottling `#3B322A`, dusted grey-white with old mould bloom `#9A9384`,
cracked in a fine dry web. The rim is smoother and slightly glossy where it was
turned. Through the cracks: **pale straw paste** `#E8D9A8` — the only warm
colour on the whole exterior, and the thing that makes a soft spot legible.

**Surface detail is everything.** The crust is 6 units thick, which means it
**cannot carry surface noise** [X4] — the silhouette must be smooth. So the
Wheel's character is entirely texture: the mould ridges, the weave of the cloth
it was bandaged in, the bloom dust, the crack web. A concept artist should treat
this as a material study, not a rock formation.

**The soft spots** [N2]. Multiple patches scattered around the perimeter where
the rind has gone soft and damp: discoloured olive-brown, swollen into low
bulges, wet-sheened where nothing else on the wheel has any sheen, pale paste
bulging through widened cracks, thin dark veins converging into them, mite pits
scattered like a rash. Each **visible at ~15 m, invisible beyond** — the crew
swims the curve with a lamp, finds multiple candidates, chooses one. The search
is the level design.

**The breach.** A hole one rat wide, ragged, chips and dust still hanging in the
water, pale paste-white against near-black. Twenty seconds of noise. Then, on the
inside, nothing to mark it — the inner face is pale, uniformly gnawed, utterly
featureless. The crew must mark the hole with a light stick or lose it.

**Don't draw.** Red wax. Any lumpy or rocky silhouette. Anything that makes it
read as a wall rather than an object — if you can't tell it's a wheel of cheese,
the shot has failed.

### ▸ THE DARK VEINS — *the drifting roquefort trail*
`r 226 → 100 · water · scatter · part: roquefort float, graded inward, rotating`

**What it is.** Everything inside the Great Wheel down to the second seal. You
come through the crust into **absolute black** — no ambient light, no floor, no
horizon, nothing for a lamp to land on — and the only things that exist are
**blue vein-glows inside floating chunks of roquefort**. They are lanterns, and
the trail between them is the entire level design.

**The frame.** The signature image of the middle game: a single dark mass
hanging in a void, lit from within by a filigree of cold blue, with a rat
silhouetted against it and three more glows receding into the black behind,
growing from fist-sized to house-sized. The chunks **slowly rotate on their own
axes**, so the glowing edges turn away into darkness and come back on their own
time. No walls. No up. No certainty.

**Palette.** Ninety percent void black `#04060A`. The chunks are almost
invisible — a near-black silhouette `#0F1218` you only read because the veins
inside are `#4FB8C8`, cooling to `#1E3A5F` where they diffuse into the paste.
Helmet-lamp white is the only other colour, and it should look **useless**: a
cone that reaches four metres into nothing.

**Light & fog.** `ambient: 0`, and mean it. Fog `range 0.55`, colour `#0A0D12`.
The glows should be visible *through* the fog when the chunk itself is not. When
a chunk rotates, the glow turns away; the screen goes black for a moment, then
blue returns.

**The gradient — draw it.** Chunks start fist-sized and sparse at the wheel's
inner face, grow to boulders, then house-sized as you go deeper, tightening and
densifying. That escalation is how the player is funnelled without instruction,
and in a wide shot it reads as **a trail of lights getting bigger toward
something solid**.

**The parts.**
- **Floating roquefort** — dark, wet, cold; the vein network is *surface-visible
  and emissive*, lit from inside like a paper lantern, throwing faint blue onto
  anything passing. Each chunk rotates slowly on its own axis.
- **Openings** — some chunks have hand-carved mouths and small air pockets
  inside, so the trail is also a reason to stop. Draw one with a rat half inside,
  lit blue from within, lamp off.
- **Dead-end chunks** — two or three off the main line, same glow, no next chunk
  beyond. The crew swims up to nothing.
- **The trail terminus** — the last several chunks grow massive and cluster
  densely, and then the trail ends. What you see beyond is not more chunks — it's
  a curved wall so large it fills the fog, dark, featureless, impossibly solid.

**Don't draw.** Any ambient light. Any floor. Any two chunks at the same size
(the sizes should always escalate). The trail must always be legible as a
direction until it ends.

### ▸ THE MELT SHELL — *the second gate* · hidden entrance
`r 96 → 91 · sphere, undrillable · part: melt rind`

**What it is.** The trail ends at a curved wall that cannot be breached: the
outer shell of a massive central cheese body containing everything from the Melt
inward. **Undrillable everywhere.** The only way through is one **obscured,
recessed entrance** found because the vein trail leads directly to it.

**The frame — the wall.** Four rats staring up at near-infinite curve. Their
lamps reach a few metres, then nothing. The wall surface is scorched, cracked
with deep heat-fissures bleeding orange light, cold-black between them. No
detail. No decoration. Just mass and heat-scarring. Scale: **colossal**.

**The frame — finding it.** Close approach, lamp-lit, a recessed crevice barely
wider than a rat, the last glowing vein-filaments converging into it. The
entrance is angled, so it reads only from one approach. At any other angle the
wall looks featureless. Scale: **intimate**.

**Palette.** Scorched dark brown `#3A2A1A`, almost black `#1A1414`, cracked to
show deep heat-cracks glowing burnt orange `#C44A1A` from within. **No blue
here** — no veins on the shell exterior, only where they led *to* it.

**Light & fog.** Standard fog thins a bit here — the last stretch before
entering. Shell surface is matte, heat-scarred, giving back no reflectivity.
Interior glow is orange and faint, like looking into a crack in hot coal.

**The parts.**
- **The shell** — a continuous closed surface, uninterrupted except for the one
  entrance. It represents a seal, a wall, a _no_ that cannot be negotiated. Art
  should sell this as impassable.
- **The entrance** — a narrow mouth, recessed so it doesn't read from distance,
  angled so you have to approach at exactly the right angle to see it. The last
  vein-glows point *at* it, not *into* it. Draw one rat nose-to-entrance, the
  crew approaching in file, the last rat looking back at the trail they won't see
  again.

**Don't draw.** Decoration. Texture. Weakness anywhere except the one entrance.
The shell is a flat refusal, and the entrance is the _only_ answer the game
gives you.

### ▸ THE MELT — *fondue cathedral*
`r 88 → 46 · submerged, hot · fused · part: fondue`

**What it is.** Thermal vents from the heart have cooked this layer soft. The
water is **still water, still submerged** — there is no air anywhere in this
game — but the water itself has become a spectacle: hot, orange-lit from below,
full of ribbons of molten cheese billowing off the ceiling and dissolving into
the current like plumes at a hydrothermal vent. It's the one place in the abyss
with **space**: three or four caverns 25–40 units across, and the last generous
air-pocket refill of the run — an ordinary bubble seeded near the ceiling, just
more of them than anywhere else.

**Space is the whole point.** After a biome where the lamp found nothing and
then a biome where it found a wall, this is the first room whose far side you
can see. Draw the volume first and the detail second: high ceilings, long
sightlines, every teammate visible at once. Everything dangerous here is in
plain view — that's the trade for the light. The hazards are *on*: they have a
rhythm, and the route is timed against it.

**The frame.** A rat swimming out of a narrow bore into a cathedral cavern lit
entirely from below by vents in the floor, orange light casting long shadows
upward. Ropes of molten cheese pouring off the ceiling in slow arcs, breaking
into trailing streamers and dissipating into the water, never reaching the floor
solid. A cooled floe in the foreground, dull amber crusted grey, a rat gripping
its edge for stability against the current, fins out. This is the closest thing
to *resting* in the game.

**Palette.** The full orange run: `#FFB65A` at the hottest pools, `#FF8A2B`
mid, `#B04A10` in the crust, going to dull amber `#6E3A14`, finally burnt
grey-brown where a floe has cooled. This is the only saturated biome and should
feel almost uncomfortably warm after the blue veins — but it is underwater:
particulate haze drifting in currents, water shimmer, not dry air.

**Light & fog.** `lightRange ×1.4` — you barely need your lamp. **Light comes
from below**, inverting every shadow you've seen so far. `fogDensity ×0.6`:
thinner and warmer than elsewhere, shot through with mineral haze and threads of
dissolving cheese like hydrothermal plumes. Sound carries further here — voices
work, without ever leaving water.

**The parts.**
- **Chamber walls** — cheese that has sagged: droops, strings, pools. No crisp
  edges anywhere. Reference: the underside of a raclette wheel, underwater
  lava-tube geology.
- **The falls** — molten ribbons pouring off the ceiling, breaking and
  dissolving into the current. Telegraph: sag, bulge, ribbon uncoiling into
  water.
- **The roiling pools** — bright patches on the floor, crusting and re-opening
  on a rhythm.
- **The thermal vents** — rhythmic jets of superheated water from floor cracks,
  pale gold, distorting the water like heat-haze. They blind and shove. Timed
  right, they ride a rat up a shaft — the underwater equivalent of a geyser.
- **The heat shimmer** — visible distortion where hot and cold water mix,
  quietly burning O₂. The reason this is not a free rest stop.
- **The floes** — cooled slabs, dull amber crusted grey, climbable footing in
  open water. The route across is partly built from what already fell.
- **A coated rat** — slathered orange, movement fouled, another rat scraping it
  off. Comic and horrible: the game's register.

**Don't draw.** Fire, embers, dry smoke, lava. No surface, no sky, no rat out of
the water. This biome never leaves the swim. Hot dairy in a hot current, not
volcanism — it should look *delicious* and be awful.

### ▸ THE GALLERIES — *mite-bored paste*
`r 50 → 6 · fused · part: mite bore`

**What it is.** The Gouda's shell and the last act. One colossal mass bored
through by mites into thousands of holes. **Not a cave system — a sponge.**

**The frame.** Looking down a one-rat bore that opens, without warning, into a
chamber with fourteen other bores in its walls, all identical. Four rats in the
chamber, each lamp pointing at a different hole. One light stick already dropped,
lime green against warm tan, and it's the only distinguishable thing in the
image.

**Palette.** Warm mid-tan `#C69B5E` in the lamp, falling to `#7A5A32` at range.
Fine ochre dust hanging in every beam. It is monotone on purpose: after the
melt's orange and the veins' blue, arriving somewhere with **no colour
information** is the point.

**Light & fog.** Lamp only, standard range, dust-heavy. The tunnels are close
enough that the lamp always reaches the wall — you can always see, and it never
helps.

**The parts.**
- **The bores** — round-section tunnels barely wider than a rat, walls dry,
  matte, scalloped with tool-mark-like gouges from whatever ate them. They bend
  three times in their length so you never see far.
- **The chambers** — every eighth chunk or so opens into a room 8–20 u across
  with a dozen bores in its walls at all angles, including the floor and ceiling.
  These are where the crew regroups, argues, and realises nobody marked the way
  in.
- **The squeeze** — the frame that sells the biome: a bore too narrow to haul
  the Gouda through, a rat holding it, and a decision.
- **Light sticks** — by this point in the run there should be a trail of them,
  and in an Ascent shot, a trail that doesn't go where you remember.

**Don't draw.** Any two frames that look different. The horror of this biome is
that it's all the same picture.

---

### ▸ THE HEART — *fresh curd*
`r 8 → 0 · submerged chamber · one air pocket · part: fresh curd`

**What it is.** One **underwater chamber** at the exact centre. The only place
in the abyss that is being **made** rather than decaying — because the
Duplicator never stopped [L2]. Inside this chamber: **one final air pocket**
(a bubble at the ceiling), and the **Golden Gouda** at the centre.

**The frame.** A bore opens in a wall that is suddenly white and wet. Behind it:
a rounded **underwater chamber**, walls of soft, wet forming curd, whey beading
and running, warm light emanating from a single source at the floor-centre — the
**Golden Gouda**, a small wheel of impossible gold. The last air pocket floats
near the ceiling as a bubble in the water, clearly separate from the water
below. Four exhausted rats in the doorway, weightless, finally still.

**Palette.** Curd white `#F4F0E4` shading to cream, wet highlights everywhere,
lit gold `#FFE9A8` from the centre. After 400 units of black, blue, orange and
tan, this is the first clean white in the game and it should read as *holy*, and
faintly obscene. Water still water: slightly diffusing the light, not air.

**Light & fog.** The Gouda is the light. Warm, wide, soft-edged — the opposite
of every lamp cone ever seen. Minimal fog inside the chamber (the bubble air is
clear), but the chamber is still submerged: rats are still swimming. Water, not
air.

**The parts.**
- **Fresh curd** — soft, wet, luminous at the edges, still separating and
  forming. Sticky: the drag modifier is diegetic, and surfaces should look like
  they'd hold onto you.
- **The air pocket** — a bubble at the chamber ceiling, clearly a bubble *in*
  water, not an air-filled room. Draw it as a meniscus, a silver surface seen
  from below, slightly smaller than it was 400 units ago.
- **The Golden Gouda** — a small wheel, gold, seamless, warm, humming quietly.
  Not ornate, not mechanical, no visible technology. It looks like a cheese that
  happens to be made of light.
- **Set dressing** — the crews who came this far: a shrine at the water line of
  the chamber wall, a helmet, chalk that the curd is slowly swallowing, marked
  by slow time.

**Don't draw.** Machinery. Runes. Anything that explains what the Duplicator is.
Never draw air instead of water, even here.

## 3. Recurring elements

**The rat diver.** Brass diving helmet with a round face-port, canvas suit,
weighted boots, small O₂ bottle, a belt of light sticks. The helmet lamp is a
hard white cone that is always the wrong length for the room. Silhouette should
read at 40 m in fog: **round head, narrow body, long tail** — the tail is the
whole silhouette and should be visible in every pose.

**The bell.** Iron, riveted, one caged bulb, chalk-covered inside [L3]. Warm
orange interior light spilling out of the hatch is the only friendly image in the
game, and it should appear exactly twice: Beat 1, and the last frame of a
successful run.

**The driller.** Heavy, two-handed, ugly, industrial — visibly the wrong tool
for delicate work. Occupies the same hands as the Gouda [D20], so any frame with
a rat holding both is a frame with a lie in it.

**Light sticks.** Chemical lime `#C6FF4A`, flat, no warmth, no falloff
character — deliberately alien against every cheese colour. They are the only
mark a player leaves on the world, and in art they should always mean *somebody
made a decision here*.

**The catfish** [E4, G9]. An open-water animal: massive, pale-bellied, lure
lights, drawn to drill noise and to the Gouda's glow. On the descent it is a
shape at the edge of the lamp. After the pickup it is in the frame.

**The Gouda's light on a carrier** [G4]. The carrier's own lamp is off, so they
are lit *by what they're holding*: gold from below, face-port glowing, the
brightest object in any group shot and therefore the most obviously doomed.

## 4. The Ascent — the same map, wrong

The pickup flips the world [C4]. Concept art should be produced in **pairs**:
the descent frame, and the same location on the way out.

| Location | Descent | Ascent |
|---|---|---|
| The heart | warm, gold, the bubble at ceiling | dark, cold, the chamber stopped forming, the bubble is gone — used |
| The galleries | dust in the lamp, silence | your light-stick trail, and gaps where you were sure you left one |
| The melt | falls slow, rhythmic, transparent vents | falls faster, pools wider, the Gouda's gold **camouflaged** against orange — your beacon doesn't help |
| The melt shell | the impassable wall | (passed once, never approached again) |
| The dark veins | rotating chunks, trail growing denser | chunks rotating against you, glows going out behind you — the trail was never marked backward |
| The Great Wheel | the soft spots, hard to find | the breach hole glowing faint with the light stick you left, and something *outside* it |
| The drift | empty, pale, harmless | **occupied** [E2] — a shadow at lamp's edge |
| The bell | hatch open, warm interior light | thirty metres away, past the boundary where a dropped Gouda is gone forever [G7] |

**The last air pocket is spent** — used at the heart. Every other bubble the
descent filled is now a hollow pocket, a relic, visibly wrong. The way home
should look *emptier* and more hostile than the way in.

## 5. Shot list for concept art

Ordered by what's most worth having first.

1. **"The Wheel"** — the approach, three-quarter view, rats as dust motes, soft
   spots scattered around the perimeter. The game's key art.
2. **"The soft spot"** — mid-shot of one weeping, swollen bulge, one rat and a
