# The Map — visual bible

Companion to `plan-mvp.md` (what gets built) and `cheese-parts.md` (the numbers
that build it). **This document is for the eye.** It describes the run from the
bell to the heart as a sequence of images, so that concept art, materials and
lighting can be produced without re-deriving them from generator parameters.

Everything here is downstream of decisions in `idea-register.md`; the tags in
brackets point back at them.

---

## 0. The rules of the look

**The abyss is made of cheese, and nobody explains why.** [L2] The Golden Gouda
is a duplicator that never stopped running, so the world is a single organism of
cheese with an **age gradient**: the outside is centuries dead — bleached, dry,
crusted, colonised — and the centre is warm, wet, and still being made. Every
material decision should be readable as *how far from the heart is this*.

**Five colours, spent on traits, not on zones.** [U7] Fog eats everything else.
The whole map is built from these and their neutrals:

| Colour | Hex | Means | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| bone / ash | `#CFC8BA` → `#8A8579` | dead cheese, harmless | the drift |
| near-black rind | `#1A1714` → `#3B322A` | the wall you cannot dig | the Great Wheel |
| cold vein blue | `#4FB8C8` → `#1E3A5F` | route, and the only light | the dark veins, both phases |
| molten orange | `#FF8A2B` → `#B04A10` | heat, refuge, and burns | the melt |
| warm tan | `#C69B5E` → `#7A5A32` | the labyrinth, endless and same | the galleries |
| **gold-white** | `#FFE9A8` / `#FFF6DE` | *the prize, and nothing else* | the heart, the Gouda |

Two player-made colours cut across all of it and must never be confused with the
world: **light-stick lime** `#C6FF4A` (chemical, flat, no falloff character) and
**helmet-lamp white** `#FFF4E2` (a hard cone, always the wrong length).

**Scale rhythm.** [W14] `small → colossal → medium → small → colossal →
intimate`. Scale contrast does as much work as material contrast and costs
nothing. Every biome needs one object in frame that establishes it: a rat is
~0.6 u across, a tunnel is ~1 u, a chamber is 8–20 u, the Wheel is **466 u**.

**Light is scarce and always motivated.** Sunlight does not exist. There are
five light sources in the entire game — helmet lamps, light sticks, the veins,
the melt, and the Gouda — and each has a distinct colour, falloff and behaviour.
If something is lit, name what lit it.

**No UI in any concept art.** [D4] No markers, no map, no compass except the
diegetic one. The player's only navigational aids are silhouettes [N9] and the
light sticks they spent.

**Everything is chewed.** Mite bores, gnaw marks, mould bloom, crack webs. This
world was eaten, not built. The one exception is the heart, which is being made.

## 1. The run, start to centre

Ten beats. Each is a frame worth drawing.

### Beat 1 — Inside the bell
Four rats in brass helmets in a cramped iron bathyscaphe. The only light is a
caged bulb. On the wall you wake up facing: **chalk** [L3] — a crude map, a list
of rules in four different hands, "DON'T EAT IT" scratched out three times,
tally marks for crews that came back. Warm, dry, orange-lit, claustrophobic. It
is the last comfortable image in the run.

### Beat 2 — The berth
Hatch opens. Black water. The bell hangs from nothing, its own lamp making a
small pool of light on the hull, and beyond it: nothing at all, in every
direction. Scale: **intimate**.

### Beat 3 — The drift
Pale chunks of dead cheese tumbling slowly out of the dark, far apart, lit only
by helmet lamps sweeping past them. Somewhere in here, a **wrecked bell** [W10]
with a dead diver still in the harness and a **driller** in his hands. Scale:
**small**, and it teaches you the size of things right before it lies to you.

### Beat 4 — The Wheel appears
The money shot. [D1] The lamps pick up a curve that doesn't end. Pan up, pan
down — it goes past the fog in both directions. It is not a wall; it's an
**object**, a classic wheel of cheese 466 units across, lying tilted in the
abyss, its flat face turned partly toward you so the concentric mould ridges
read as an impossible bullseye. Scale: **colossal**. This is the frame the whole
game is sold on.

### Beat 5 — The soft spot, and the breach
A search along a horizon. Then: one patch of the near-black rind gone **wet** —
discoloured, swollen, pale paste showing through the crack web, fine veins
converging into it, mite pits like a rash [N2]. The driller goes in. Twenty
seconds of the loudest noise in the game, chips and dust boiling off in the lamp
beam, and a hole one rat wide [N1]. The crew goes through single file.

### Beat 6 — Through the hole: the lanterns
Inside the husk, and it is **pitch black**. [D2b] No ambient light, no floor, no
horizon, no sense of the space at all — the lamp beam finds nothing to land on.
What you see instead: **two or three faint blue lights hanging in the dark**,
small, cold, and far apart. They are veins glowing inside floating chunks of
roquefort. Behind you, the inner face of the crust arcs away, pale and
**uniformly gnawed** in every direction — visually busy and completely
featureless [W18], which is why your breach hole is gone the moment you turn
around unless somebody spent a light stick on it [D24].

### Beat 7 — Following the lanterns
The trail. You swim to a chunk, and from it you can just make out the next one.
Each is a dark rock lit from within by a blue filigree, hanging in nothing. They
get **bigger and closer together** as you go in — first fist-sized glows at the
edge of sight, then boulders, then house-sized masses whose vein networks light
your whole crew as you pass. Nobody tells you which way is in; the trail simply
tightens. Scale: **small → medium**, deliberately climbing.

### Beat 8 — The mass
The trail ends at something that fills the fog in every direction — one
continuous body of roquefort, its veins bleeding out through the surface at a
few points where they break through. You find a mouth and go **inside**, and
from here to the heart you are within the cheese [D2]. Lamps reach a third as
far, the water is sticky, and the branching blue graph in the walls is the
route, the light and the only thing you can dig by hand. Scale: **colossal**,
then tight.

### Beat 9 — The melt
A bore opens into a void the water doesn't leave — you're still fully
submerged, but the water itself has caught fire with colour: a cavern forty
units across, hot, orange, **lit from its own floor**, with ropes of molten
cheese billowing off the ceiling and dissolving into the current like ink,
pooling in bright roiling patches below. You can see the far wall. You can see
every teammate. It is the last big lungful of air in the game [S10] — an
ordinary air pocket, just more of them than anywhere else. Scale: **colossal**
— and the only warm, lit, loud place in the map.

### Beat 10 — The galleries, then the heart
Into the tight water again, into a sponge: thousands of bores through warm tan
cheese, one-rat squeezes opening without warning into rooms, every surface
identical in every direction. You navigate by the light sticks you had the sense
to drop. Then a bore ends and the wall ahead is **white and wet**, and behind it
the heart: warm, luminous, still forming, whey beading off the walls — and in
the middle, the only pure gold in the world. Scale: **intimate**.

Then you pick it up, and everything goes wrong [C4].

## 2. The stops, in detail

Each stop lists the frame, the palette, the light, the parts in it, and what
*not* to draw.

---

### ▸ THE DRIFT — *bleached rind*
`r 300 → 240 · water · scatter · part: drift crumb`

**What it is.** The harmless baseline. Nothing here can hurt you and nothing
here is a wall. Its job is to establish scale honestly, so the Wheel can break
it.

**The frame.** Wide, mostly empty. Three or four pale chunks at different
distances, a rat silhouetted against the nearest one, the wreck at the edge of
lamp range with one light still weakly on.

**Palette.** Bone `#CFC8BA`, ash `#8A8579`, against water black `#05070A`.
Helmet-lamp cones are the only warmth.

**Light & fog.** Standard: lamp range nominal, fog thick enough that a chunk is
silhouette-only past ~40 m. Nothing is lit that a rat didn't light.

**The parts.**
- **Drift crumb** — irregular blocks 8–16 u, sea-glass-rounded edges, matte,
  faintly powdery, zero specular. Shallow round eyes and pores, no interior worth
  entering. Each tumbles slowly on its own axis, none in sync. Reference: sun-
  bleached driftwood and chalk cliff fall, not cheese-shop cheese.
- **The wreck** [W10, D23] — an older bell, split, half its hull gone, a diver
  still in the harness with the driller. The set dressing that carries the lore.

**Don't draw.** Anything that reads as architecture, or a chunk big enough to be
a landmark. Nothing in the drift may compete with Beat 4.

### ▸ THE GREAT WHEEL — *dark rind* · **the gate**
`r 236 → 230 · 6 u thick · hull · part: dark rind`

**What it is.** One classic wheel of cheese, 466 units across, aged in the dark
for centuries and **eaten hollow from within** [D2b] — a husk with a few metres
of crust left. The single gate of the MVP.

**The frame — exterior.** Approach from three-quarters so the flat face and the
rim are both visible. The **concentric ridges of the mould** on the flat face,
turning slowly out of the fog. Rats are dust-motes against it. Lamp beams die
long before they reach the top of the curve.

**Palette.** Near-black `#1A1714` at distance, resolving up close into deep
grey-brown mottling `#3B322A`, dusted grey-white with old mould bloom `#9A9384`,
cracked in a fine dry web. The rim is smoother and slightly glossy where it was
turned. Through the cracks: **pale straw paste** `#E8D9A8` — the only warm
colour on the whole exterior, and the thing that makes a soft spot legible.

**Surface detail is everything.** The crust is 6 units thick, which means it
**cannot carry surface noise** [X4] — the silhouette must be smooth. So the
Wheel's character is entirely texture: the mould ridges, the weave of the cloth
it was bandaged in, the bloom dust, the crack web. A concept artist should treat
this as a material study, not a rock formation.

**The soft spot** [N2]. One patch where the rind has gone soft and damp:
discoloured olive-brown, swollen into a low bulge, wet-sheened where nothing else
on the wheel has any sheen, pale paste bulging through a widened crack, thin
dark veins converging into it, mite pits scattered like a rash. **Visible at
~15 m, invisible beyond** — draw it as something you'd only find by swimming the
curve with a lamp.

**The breach.** A hole one rat wide, ragged, chips and dust still hanging in the
water, pale paste-white against near-black. Then, on the inside, nothing to mark
it.

**Don't draw.** Red wax. Any lumpy or rocky silhouette. Anything that makes it
read as a wall rather than an object — if you can't tell it's a wheel of cheese,
the shot has failed.

### ▸ THE DARK VEINS · PHASE 1 — *the drifting veins*
`r 230 → 150 · water · scatter · part: roquefort, graded inward`

**What it is.** The inside of the gate, and the first half of the dark biome.
You come through the crust into **absolute black** — no ambient, no floor, no
horizon, nothing for a lamp to land on — and the only things that exist are the
blue vein-glows inside floating chunks of roquefort. **They are lanterns, and
the trail between them is the entire level design.**

**The frame.** The signature image of the middle game: a single dark mass
hanging in a void, lit from within by a filigree of cold blue, with a rat
silhouetted against it and three more glows receding into the black behind at
decreasing sizes. No walls. No up.

**Palette.** Ninety percent void black `#04060A`. The chunks are almost
invisible — a near-black silhouette `#0F1218` you only read because the veins
inside are `#4FB8C8` cooling to `#1E3A5F` where they diffuse through the paste.
Helmet-lamp white is the only other colour, and it should look **useless**:
a cone that reaches four metres into nothing.

**Light & fog.** `ambient: 0`, and mean it. Fog `range 0.55`, colour `#0A0D12`.
The glows should be visible *through* the fog when the chunk itself is not.

**The gradient — draw it.** The chunks start fist-sized and far apart at the
crust, and grow to house-sized and close together as you go in. That escalation
is how the player is funnelled without a single instruction, and in any wide
shot it should read as **a trail of lights getting bigger toward something**.

**The parts.**
- **Floating roquefort** — dark, wet, cold; the vein network is *surface-visible
  and emissive*, so it lights its own chunk from inside like a paper lantern and
  throws faint blue onto anything passing.
- **Openings** — a few have hand-carved mouths and small air pockets inside, so
  the trail is also a reason to stop. Draw one with a rat half inside it, lit
  blue from within, lamp off.
- **The gnawed inner face** [W18] — behind you, when you look back: pale,
  uniformly chewed, thousands of overlapping bite-scallops, no feature that
  differs from any other. Busy *and* featureless. One lime light stick on it,
  and that's your door.
- **Dead-end chunks** — two or three off the main line, indistinguishable until
  you arrive and find no next glow beyond them.

**Don't draw.** Any ambient light. Any floor. Any two chunks at the same size.
The trail must always be legible as a direction.

### ▸ THE DARK VEINS · PHASE 2 — *the vein mass*
`r 150 → 90 · fused · part: roquefort`

**What it is.** The trail ends at one continuous body of roquefort that fills
the fog in every direction, and you go **inside** it. From here to the heart
there is no water between parts — you are within the cheese [D2].

**The frame — the arrival.** Wide: four small lamps against a wall of darkness
with blue veins bleeding out through its surface at a handful of points, one of
them a mouth big enough to enter. The scale jump from the last chunk to this
should be brutal.

**The frame — inside.** A tunnel of near-black paste, walls close, a lamp
reaching maybe four metres before it gives up — and running through walls,
ceiling and floor, a branching graph of cold blue light receding further than
the lamp ever reaches. The composition is a **road made of light in a room with
no light**.

**Palette.** Paste near-black with a cold cast, `#14161C` → `#2A2E38`. Veins
`#4FB8C8` at the core falling to `#1E3A5F` in the surrounding paste. No warm
colour anywhere except the rats — which is exactly what makes the melt land.

**Light & fog.** `lightRange ×0.35`, `fogDensity ×2.5`, drag ×1.25. The water is
visibly thick: spores and mould particles hanging in every beam, drifting
sideways as a rat passes. Movement should look laboured.

**The parts.**
- **The mass** — fused, so the space reads as *carved*, not assembled. Surfaces
  slightly moist, cold, blue-shadowed; the paste crumbles at the vein edges.
- **The mouths** — where veins converge and break the surface. The same visual
  logic as the Wheel's soft spot at a tenth the scale, and hand-carvable rather
  than drilled.
- **The veins** — thick as a rat's arm at the trunks, hairline at the tips,
  brightening as you approach. Where a tunnel has been hand-carved along one, the
  cut face shows the vein in cross-section, glowing brighter at the wound.
- **Fault lines** — brittle vein runs that collapse behind you: draw one
  mid-collapse, dust and slabs, blue light snuffing out section by section.

**Don't draw.** Any general fill light. If the veins aren't the only thing you
can see, the biome doesn't exist.

### ▸ THE MELT — *fondue*
`r 90 → 45 · flooded, hot · fused · part: fondue`

**What it is.** Thermal vents from the heart have cooked this layer soft.
**There is no air anywhere in this game** — the melt is still fully
submerged — but the water itself has become a spectacle: hot, orange-lit from
below, full of ribbons of molten cheese billowing off the ceiling and
dissolving into the current. It's the one place in the abyss with **space**:
three or four caverns 25–40 units across, loud with drill-worthy noise carrying
further in open water, and the last generous air-pocket refill in the run.

**Space is the whole point.** After a biome where the lamp found nothing and
then a biome where it found a wall four metres away, this is a room whose far
side you can see. Draw the volume first and the detail second: high ceilings,
long sightlines, every teammate visible at once. Everything dangerous here is in
plain view — that's the trade for the light. Draw it like an underwater
hydrothermal vent field, not a dry cave: everything drifts, billows and
disperses, nothing simply falls.

**The frame.** A rat swimming out of a flooded bore into a cavern the size of a
cathedral, lit entirely from below by vents in the floor. Ropes of molten
cheese pouring off the ceiling in slow arcs, breaking into trailing streamers
and dissipating into the water like smoke from a chimney, never reaching the
floor as a solid stream. A cooled floe in the foreground, a rat gripping its
edge for stability against the current, fins out, weightless — the closest
thing to *resting* in the whole game, and still visibly floating.

**Palette.** The full orange run: `#FFB65A` at the hottest pools, `#FF8A2B`
mid, `#B04A10` in the cooling crust, going to dull amber `#6E3A14` and finally
a burnt grey-brown where a floe has gone cold. This is the only saturated biome
in the game and it should feel almost uncomfortably warm after the veins — but
the water is still water: particulate haze drifting in every current, not dry
air.

**Light & fog.** `lightRange ×1.4` — you barely need your lamp. **Light comes
from below**, which inverts every shadow the player has seen so far and is the
single most useful thing about the frame. `fogDensity ×0.6`: thinner and warmer
than the drift's fog, shot through with drifting mineral haze and threads of
dissolving cheese, like the shimmer around a hydrothermal vent. The open volume
carries sound further than the cramped veins did — this is where voices carry,
without ever leaving the water.

**The parts.**
- **Chamber walls** — cheese that has sagged: everything droops, strings, and
  pools. No crisp edges anywhere. Reference: the underside of a raclette wheel,
  and underwater lava-tube/pillow-basalt geology, in that order.
- **The falls** — molten ribbons pouring off the ceiling, breaking up and
  dissolving into the current rather than dripping through air. Draw the
  telegraph: a sag, a bulge, then the ribbon uncoiling into the water.
- **The pools** — bright, roiling patches on the floor, crusting over at the
  edges and re-opening.
- **The thermal vents** — rhythmic jets of superheated water and dissolved
  cheese from floor cracks, pale gold against the orange, distorting the water
  around them like a heat-haze. They blind and shove; timed right, they ride a
  rat up a shaft. The one hazard that is also an elevator — the underwater
  equivalent of a geyser, not a vent of gas.
- **The heat shimmer** — visible distortion where hot and cold water mix.
  Purely atmospheric to look at, quietly burning O₂, and the reason this isn't a
  free rest stop.
- **The floes** — cooled slabs on the floor, dull amber crusted grey, climbable
  footing in open water, overlapping. The route across a cavern floor is partly
  built from what already fell.
- **A coated rat** — slathered orange, movement fouled, another rat scraping it
  off. Comic and horrible at once, which is the game's whole register.

**Don't draw.** Fire, embers, dry smoke, or anything that reads as open-air lava
rather than cheese billowing through hot water. No surface, no sky, no rat out
of the water — this biome never leaves the swim. It's hot dairy in a hot
current, not volcanism; it should look *delicious* and be awful.

### ▸ THE GALLERIES — *mite-bored paste*
`r 50 → 6 · fused · part: mite bore`

**What it is.** The Gouda's shell and the last act. One colossal mass bored
through by mites into thousands of holes. **Not a cave system — a sponge.**

**The frame.** Looking down a one-rat bore that opens, without warning, into a
chamber with fourteen other bores in its walls, all identical. Four rats in the
chamber, each lamp pointing at a different hole. One light stick already dropped,
lime green against warm tan, and it's the only distinguishable thing in the
image.

**Palette.** Warm mid-tan `#C69B5E` in the lamp, falling to `#7A5A32` at range.
Fine ochre dust hanging in every beam. It is monotone on purpose: after the
melt's orange and the veins' blue, arriving somewhere with **no colour
information** is the point.

**Light & fog.** Lamp only, standard range, dust-heavy. The tunnels are close
enough that the lamp always reaches the wall — you can always see, and it never
helps.

**The parts.**
- **The bores** — round-section tunnels barely wider than a rat, walls dry,
  matte, scalloped with tool-mark-like gouges from whatever ate them. They bend
  three times in their length so you never see far.
- **The chambers** — every eighth chunk or so opens into a room 8–20 u across
  with a dozen bores in its walls at all angles, including the floor and ceiling.
  These are where the crew regroups, argues, and realises nobody marked the way
  in.
- **The squeeze** — the frame that sells the biome: a bore too narrow to haul
  the Gouda through, a rat holding it, and a decision.
- **Light sticks** — by this point in the run there should be a trail of them,
  and in an Ascent shot, a trail that doesn't go where you remember.

**Don't draw.** Any two frames that look different. The horror of this biome is
that it's all the same picture.

### ▸ THE HEART — *fresh curd*
`r 6 → 0 · one air pocket · part: fresh curd`

**What it is.** One chamber at the centre. The only place in the abyss that is
being **made** rather than decaying — because the Duplicator never stopped
[L2]. And the last air pocket in the game.

**The frame.** A bore ends in a wall that is suddenly white and wet. Through it:
a rounded chamber, walls of soft forming curd, whey beading and running, warm
light from a single source at the centre — the **Golden Gouda**, a small wheel of
impossible gold, lighting the whole room like a hearth. Four exhausted rats in
the doorway.

**Palette.** Curd white `#F4F0E4` shading to cream, wet highlights everywhere,
lit gold `#FFE9A8` from the centre. After 400 units of black, blue, orange and
tan, this is the first clean white in the game and it should read as *holy*, and
faintly obscene.

**Light & fog.** The Gouda is the light. Warm, wide, soft-edged — the opposite
of every lamp cone the player has seen. Almost no fog: the air pocket is clear.

**The parts.**
- **Fresh curd** — soft, wet, luminous at the edges, still separating. Sticky:
  the drag modifier is diegetic, and the surfaces should look like they'd hold
  onto you.
- **The Golden Gouda** — a small wheel, gold, seamless, warm, humming. Not
  ornate, not mechanical, no visible technology. It looks like a cheese that
  happens to be made of light.
- **Set dressing** — the crews who got this far and no further: a shrine, a
  helmet, chalk on the curd wall that the curd is slowly swallowing.

**Don't draw.** Machinery. Runes. Anything that explains what the Duplicator is.

## 3. Recurring elements

**The rat diver.** Brass diving helmet with a round face-port, canvas suit,
weighted boots, small O₂ bottle, a belt of light sticks. The helmet lamp is a
hard white cone that is always the wrong length for the room. Silhouette should
read at 40 m in fog: **round head, narrow body, long tail** — the tail is the
whole silhouette and should be visible in every pose.

**The bell.** Iron, riveted, one caged bulb, chalk-covered inside [L3]. Warm
orange interior light spilling out of the hatch is the only friendly image in the
game, and it should appear exactly twice: Beat 1, and the last frame of a
successful run.

**The driller.** Heavy, two-handed, ugly, industrial — visibly the wrong tool
for delicate work. Occupies the same hands as the Gouda [D20], so any frame with
a rat holding both is a frame with a lie in it.

**Light sticks.** Chemical lime `#C6FF4A`, flat, no warmth, no falloff
character — deliberately alien against every cheese colour. They are the only
mark a player leaves on the world, and in art they should always mean *somebody
made a decision here*.

**The catfish** [E4, G9]. An open-water animal: massive, pale-bellied, lure
lights, drawn to drill noise and to the Gouda's glow. On the descent it is a
shape at the edge of the lamp. After the pickup it is in the frame.

**The Gouda's light on a carrier** [G4]. The carrier's own lamp is off, so they
are lit *by what they're holding*: gold from below, face-port glowing, the
brightest object in any group shot and therefore the most obviously doomed.

## 4. The Ascent — the same map, wrong

The pickup flips the world [C4]. Concept art should be produced in **pairs**:
the descent frame, and the same location on the way out.

| Location | Descent | Ascent |
|---|---|---|
| The heart | warm, gold, luminous | dark, cold, the walls stopped forming |
| The galleries | dust in the lamp, silence | your light-stick trail, and gaps where you were sure you left one |
| The melt | falls slow and telegraphed | falls faster, pools wider, and the Gouda's gold is **camouflaged** against the orange — the one place your beacon doesn't help |
| The vein mass | blue road receding into the dark | veins dying back section by section ahead of you |
| The drifting veins | a trail of lanterns leading inward | the lanterns going out one by one behind you — and the trail was never marked for the return |
| The inner crust | one lime light stick marking your door | the same light stick, and something between you and it |
| The drift | empty, harmless | occupied [E2] |
| The bell | hatch open, warm | thirty metres away, past the boundary where a dropped Gouda is gone for good [G7] |

**Air pockets stay popped** [S11] — visibly deflated, collapsed, obviously
already used. The way home should look *drier* than the way in.

## 5. Shot list for concept art

Ordered by what's most worth having first.

1. **"The Wheel"** — the approach, three-quarter view, rats as dust motes. The
   game's key art.
2. **"The soft spot"** — mid-shot of the weeping bulge, one rat and a lamp, the
   rest of the wheel featureless around it.
3. **"The lanterns"** — the trail: one vein-lit chunk close, a rat silhouetted
   against it, three smaller glows receding into total black. The middle game's
   key image, and the second most important frame in the document.
4. **"The gnawed face"** — looking back at the inner crust, one light stick
   marking the hole, void black in every other direction.
5. **"The mass"** — the arrival: four small lamps against something that fills
   the fog, veins bleeding out through its surface, one mouth to enter.
6. **"The blue road"** — inside a vein tunnel, lamp defeated, the graph
   receding further than any light reaches.
7. **"Into the light"** — a rat swimming out of a dark bore into the melt's open
   water, orange light from below, the far wall of the cavern visible behind.
8. **"Fourteen holes"** — the galleries chamber, four lamps pointing at four
   different bores.
9. **"The heart"** — the doorway shot, gold on white.
10. **"The carrier"** — a rat lit from below by the Gouda, own lamp off, in a
    dark tunnel, three other rats' lamps on them.
11. **"The squeeze"** — the Gouda, a bore too narrow, a decision.
12. **"The chalk wall"** — inside the bell, four hands of graffiti, "DON'T EAT
    IT" struck through three times.
13. **"The wreck"** — the drift, a dead bell, a dead diver, a working driller.
14. **"Thirty metres"** — the last stretch, the bell's hatch light, and open
    water in between.
15. **"The lanterns, going out"** — the Ascent pair for shot 3: the same trail,
    fewer glows, and the nearest one dying.
